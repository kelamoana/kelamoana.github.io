Mauka to Makai

A management game about the ahupuaʻa, the Hawaiian land division that runs from the mountain to the reef. You play the konohiki and keep water, food and people in balance across a generated island.

Role
Solo design & engineering
Stack
Godot 4, GDScript · three.js prototypes
Status
Playable in Godot, Oʻahu map in progress
Year
2026

Overview

The idea

Each moon is one turn, and your people need food. Water is the traffic, in the spirit of Mini Metro: demand keeps growing while the land and labor to meet it stay limited. You shape the land so water, food and people keep flowing from mauka to makai without breaking anything downstream.

Generating an island that drains like a real one

Layered Perlin noise and domain warping build the terrain. Northeast trade winds drop rain on windward slopes and cast rain shadows. A priority-flood fill and D8 flow routing make every cell drain to the sea, and the high-flow cells become streams. Drainage basins are merged into 7 to 14 ridge-bounded slices, each one an ahupuaʻa.

Systems that push back

Forest smooths seasonal runoff; clearing it sends silt downstream. ʻAuwai divert stream water into terraced loʻi, which trap that silt. Fishponds need the right freshwater inflow, and reef stocks regrow logistically but suffer from muddy water. Every choice upstream shows up at the reef.

Cultural care

The ahupuaʻa is living history for Native Hawaiians and still part of land and water decisions today. The project aims to involve Native Hawaiian advisors, get ʻōlelo Hawaiʻi right, and present the system as stewardship, not decoration.

What's next

Porting the simulation to Godot 4, kept separate from rendering so balance carries over, with a stylized low-poly look. Then trails that carry goods, a district layer, a moon calendar, save and load, and a real tutorial.
